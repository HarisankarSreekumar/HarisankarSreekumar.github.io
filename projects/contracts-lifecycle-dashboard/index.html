Contracts Lifecycle & Performance Analysis

Context a UK university's contracts processBuilt in Power BI · Power Query · DAXOutput two-page interactive dashboard

Overview

I turned ~6,500 raw contract records into a dimensional model and a two-page Power BI dashboard. It shows how many contracts meet a 40-working-day completion target, how large the overdue backlog is, and where in the lifecycle the largest delays sit. The goal was operational insight that helps teams decide where to allocate resources and fix the process. Built during a data analyst role; the organisation is not named and figures are rounded.

01

Business problem

A contract moves through request, allocation, initiation, progression, signature, completion and sometimes termination. Without one consolidated view, management couldn't easily answer basic operational questions:

  • How many contracts are being processed?
  • What share of completed contracts meet the 40-working-day target?
  • How many have breached it?
  • How many remain open, and how many of those are overdue?
  • Which stages contribute most to delays?
  • Do some faculties or contract types see disproportionate delays?
  • Where should teams prioritise process improvements?

03

Data preparation

Each contract has a unique CONTRACT_ID, a status, a faculty, a contract type and eight lifecycle milestone dates, from DATE_REQUESTED through DATE_ALLOCATED, DATE_INITIATED, DATE_PROGRESSED, DATE_PENDING_SIGNATURE, DATE_FULLY_SIGNED and DATE_COMPLETED to DATE_TERMINATED, plus CREATED and UPDATED record fields. Power Query prepared it before modelling:

1 · Type standardisation

Converted lifecycle milestone columns to proper date types.

2 · Missing dates

Identified missing milestone dates, mostly contracts that hadn't reached that stage yet.

3 · Quality flags

Kept missing and invalid date records where appropriate, flagged so incomplete lifecycles aren't mistaken for valid completed ones.

4 · ID validation

Checked contract identifiers for uniqueness and consistency.

5 · Lifecycle restructure

Built a separate contract-stage dataset to analyse individual milestones and stage durations.

6 · Date validation

Made sure dates supported chronological analysis and working-day calculations.

04

Data model

Five tables separate contract-level facts, lifecycle-stage events, descriptive attributes and dates. Relationships run one-to-many from dimension to fact with single-direction filtering, and the two fact tables never join directly: they connect through the shared contract dimension.

active, one-to-many (1 → *)inactive, used via DAX where neededamber = fact · grey = dimension
  • Fact_Contract stores contract-level records and performance; Fact_Contract_stages stores one row per lifecycle stage record.
  • Dim_Contracts holds contract attributes and filters both facts, so contract-level and stage-level views stay consistent.
  • Dim_Contract_stages holds stage definitions and attributes.
  • Dim_date is the one calendar. Request date is the active relationship; completion-date analysis uses an inactive relationship activated only where needed.
05

DAX & business logic

The key requirement was measuring against working days, not calendar days, and keeping completed-contract performance separate from the open backlog.

Working-day calculation

Elapsed time between lifecycle dates is calculated with NETWORKDAYS, so weekends don't count against the 40-working-day target. That gives a clean split between contracts that met the target and those that exceeded it.

SLA definitions
Within Target
Completed contracts processed in 40 working days or fewer.
Breached
Completed contracts processed in more than 40 working days.
Open
Contracts not yet completed or terminated, according to the defined status logic.
Overdue Open
Open contracts that have already passed the 40-working-day threshold.

Dashboard

Deliberately two pages: an executive summary and an investigation view, so stakeholders aren't overwhelmed.

07

Findings

SLA performance

Of assessable completed contracts, about 56% breached the 40-working-day target and about 44% met it: a substantial gap between actual and expected performance.

Processing time

Median completion was about 50 working days and the average about 82. Because the median is also above target, delays are widespread, not just a few very long contracts.

Open backlog

About two-thirds of open contracts were already past the 40-working-day threshold, so the backlog needs monitoring alongside historical completion.

Lifecycle bottleneck

The step from progressed to pending signature stood out: median about 26 days, average about 57. That points to approvals, documentation, stakeholder dependencies and internal hand-offs as places to investigate.

08

Recommendations

These are recommendations drawn from the analysis, not implemented outcomes.

  1. Review the progression-to-signature process to understand what causes the bottleneck.
  2. Prioritise overdue open contracts through an operational review with ageing-based escalation.
  3. Investigate faculty and contract-type differences to see whether some categories face recurring problems.
  4. Monitor SLA performance regularly to catch deteriorating trends early.
  5. Review how resources are allocated across the stages where delays concentrate.
  6. Agree consistent lifecycle data definitions and improve milestone date recording, so future reporting is more accurate.
